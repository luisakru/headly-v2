# ✨ Surprising facts

## The chocolate is innocent

Many people think chocolate triggers migraine. Often it is the other way round: craving sweets is one of the early warning signs. The attack has already begun, and the chocolate just happens to be there at the wrong time. The same goes for yawning, needing to pee often or sudden irritability. Knowing these signs lets you act earlier.

## Sex can help

A survey from Münster in 2013 found that for a good half of the people with migraine who had sex during an attack, the pain got better, and for some it went away completely. Probably thanks to the body's own pain relievers such as endorphins. Only if you feel like it, of course.

## Migraine on holiday

Many people get their attack not at peak stress but afterwards: on the first day of holiday, at the weekend, after the exam. One study found that the risk rises clearly in the hours after stress drops. Experts call this let-down migraine. What can help: wind down slowly after stressful phases and keep your rhythm, including sleep.

## Alice in Wonderland

During an aura, some people feel their body or things suddenly look huge or tiny, far away or very close. This is called Alice in Wonderland syndrome. Lewis Carroll, the author of "Alice in Wonderland", had migraine. Whether he described his own auras is not proven, but it is a nice guess.

## Children have it too

About one in ten school children has migraine. Attacks are often shorter, the pain is more often on both sides, and some children mainly have tummy pain. Before puberty, boys and girls are affected equally. Afterwards, women are about three times as many, probably because of hormones.

## Maybe it protects against diabetes

A large French study of almost 75,000 women found: women with active migraine developed type 2 diabetes less often. Why is unclear, and a link is not yet protection. But it is news worth remembering for a condition with so many downsides.

## You are in good company

- **Hildegard of Bingen** described shining lights and visions in the 12th century that are often read today as migraine aura
- **Charles Darwin, Friedrich Nietzsche and Virginia Woolf** had headaches that by today's view were probably migraine
- **Serena Williams** has spoken openly about her migraine

## It often gets better

For many women, migraine becomes much rarer after menopause, and it eases for many people in older age too. And in pregnancy, many women with migraine without aura have fewer attacks from the second trimester.
