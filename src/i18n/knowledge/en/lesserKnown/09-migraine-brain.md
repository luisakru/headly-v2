# 🧩 The migraine brain

Does our brain work differently? A little, yes, and also between attacks.

## It gets used to things less

When a brain hears the same sound again and again, it usually responds a little less each time. It learns: unimportant, filter it out. In people with migraine, this habituation is often missing in measurements. Stimuli arrive at full force, even the tenth time. That explains why light, noise and smells can be so tiring, even on good days.

## It reacts to change

The migraine brain likes things steady. Many triggers are really changes: getting up later than usual, skipping a meal, stress that suddenly eases, hormones that drop. That is why a regular rhythm helps many people more than any single measure.

## It runs on a tight energy budget

Measurements in migraine often show a strained energy supply in nerve cells. That is the idea behind the ketogenic diet and behind magnesium, B2 and Q10.

## And high sensitivity?

Many people with migraine describe themselves as highly sensitive: they notice more, get overwhelmed faster and need more time to withdraw. This fits the missing habituation well. To be honest: high sensitivity is a personality concept, not a diagnosis, and a direct link to migraine has hardly been studied. As a picture, it can still help you understand yourself and take your limits seriously.
