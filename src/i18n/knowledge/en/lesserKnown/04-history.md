# 🏛️ History and research

Migraine is one of the oldest known diseases. Still, there is no cure to this day. The reasons are medical, but also social.

## Known since antiquity

As early as the 2nd century, the Greek physician Aretaeus described attacks with one-sided headache, nausea and sensitivity to light. The physician Galen called it "hemicrania", half skull. Via French, this word became our "migraine".

## From nervous disorder to "women's disease"

In the 18th and 19th centuries, doctors linked migraine to nervousness and hysteria. Women were thought to have "weak nerves", so migraine became the ailment of delicate ladies, smiled at rather than taken seriously. The image of the pale woman with her hand on her forehead comes from this time.

In the 20th century, the influential US neurologist Harold Wolff described a "migraine personality": ambitious, perfectionist, tense. He claimed women with migraine did not accept their role as women, especially in bed. That put the blame on the patients themselves. It gave rise to the line "Not tonight, darling, I have a migraine", migraine as an excuse.

Today it is clear: migraine is a neurological disease with measurable changes in the brain and strong heredity. Women are affected three times as often because of hormones, not character. Still, the old prejudices linger. Many still experience their pain being played down.

## Little money for a lot of suffering

Worldwide, migraine is among the conditions that cost the most years lived with disability. Yet for a long time, research received only a fraction of what other diseases with a similar burden get. A US analysis found: per person affected, public research funding for migraine was just a few cents a year, and many times more for comparable diseases.

## Why it is so hard to treat

- **Many diseases under one name.** Over a hundred genes contribute, in a different mix for each person. What helps one person doesn't help another
- **No blood test, no scan.** Migraine is diagnosed from the description. There is no marker yet that shows which treatment suits whom
- **The brain is hard to reach.** You can hardly watch an attack directly; it comes unpredictably and is over after hours
- **Strong placebo effect.** In studies, often a third of people on a dummy pill also get better. A real medication has to clearly beat that
- **Lots of chance.** Almost all classic preventives were developed for other diseases; their effect on migraine was discovered by the way

## Things are moving

- **1920s:** ergotamine from ergot, the first targeted migraine medication
- **1991:** sumatriptan, the first triptan
- **2018:** the first CGRP antibodies, the first medications developed specifically to prevent migraine
- **2021:** the Brain Prize, one of the most important awards in brain research, goes to four researchers who discovered CGRP as a key to migraine

There is no cure yet. But migraine is more treatable today than ever before, and it is finally being taken seriously.
