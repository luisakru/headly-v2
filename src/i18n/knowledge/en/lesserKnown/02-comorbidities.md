# 🔗 Comorbidities

Migraine rarely comes alone. Some conditions are clearly more common in people with migraine. Knowing this helps you spot connections and can shape the choice of treatment.

## Depression and anxiety

The link goes both ways: migraine raises the risk of depression and anxiety, and the other way round. This is not a weakness but probably partly the same biology. Some preventives such as amitriptyline help with both.

## Thyroid

There are hints that an underactive thyroid is more common in migraine. It can be checked with a simple blood test and treated well. Ask about it if you also often feel exhausted, cold or without drive.

## Gut

Irritable bowel and other gut problems are more common in migraine. Gut and brain are in close contact through nerves and messengers; research on this is still young.

## And also

- **Allergies and asthma**
- **Restless legs:** restless legs in the evening
- **Sleep problems:** both too little and too much sleep can trigger attacks
- **A hole in the heart wall (PFO):** more common in migraine with aura, mostly harmless and only rarely a reason to act

## What you can do with this

Tell your doctor about complaints that seem to have nothing to do with your head too. Sometimes one treatment can tackle two problems. And sometimes a condition rules out certain medications, beta blockers in asthma, for example.
