# 🚴 Zone 2 training

"Do more endurance exercise" is something everyone with migraine hears at some point. Zone 2 training turns it into a plan: not just moving somehow, but in a specific, calm range, regularly and with a system.

## Exercise as prevention

A Swedish study compared endurance training directly with topiramate, a classic preventive medication. Training three times a week for 40 minutes reduced attacks just as much as the tablet. Nobody in the exercise group had side effects; in the topiramate group, a third did. Newer analyses of many studies also show: regular exercise reduces migraine days.

## What Zone 2 is

Training ranges are divided by heart rate, usually into five zones. Zone 2 is the calm endurance range: you are making an effort, but you are not out of breath.

![Five heart rate zones: zone 1 50 to 60 percent of maximum heart rate, zone 2 60 to 70, zone 3 70 to 80, zone 4 80 to 90, zone 5 90 to 100](zones)

How to find it:

- **Talk test:** you can still talk in full sentences, but no longer sing. This is the simplest and often the most accurate method
- **Heart rate:** about 60 to 70 percent of your maximum heart rate. A rough estimate is 220 minus your age. At 35 that is about 185, so Zone 2 is about 110 to 130 beats per minute
- **Nose breathing:** if you can still breathe comfortably through your nose, you are usually in the right range

The formula is imprecise; for some, the real maximum is well above or below. When in doubt, go by the talk test. If you take beta blockers, you can't go by heart rate, because they slow it down.

## Why Zone 2 suits migraine

- **Gentle on a sensitive brain.** Sudden, hard effort can trigger an attack in some people. Zone 2 builds up slowly and evenly
- **It strengthens the energy balance.** Endurance training increases the power plants of the cells, the mitochondria. This fits the idea of an energy shortage in the migraine brain, see "Migraine brain". Whether this is the key reason in migraine has not been proven
- **Less stress, better sleep.** Both are common triggers
- **Easy to stick with.** Because it doesn't hurt, you are more likely to keep going. And regularity is almost everything with migraine

To be honest: Zone 2 has not been studied as its own programme in migraine. Studies show that more intense training and strength training work too, sometimes even better. Zone 2 is a good, well-tolerated start. If it suits you, you can add intervals or strength exercises later.

## How to start

- **Three times a week for 30 to 45 minutes,** e.g. cycling, brisk walking, swimming, cross-trainer or easy jogging
- **Start small:** in the first weeks, 20 minutes is enough. Build up slowly
- **Warm up for 10 minutes,** easing into the zone instead of starting at full speed
- **Eat something beforehand and drink enough,** low blood sugar and too little fluid are typical triggers
- **At similar times if you can,** the migraine brain likes rhythm
- **Not during an attack,** movement usually makes the pain worse then. Go gently the day after
- **Patience:** like a preventive, the effect only shows after about two to three months
