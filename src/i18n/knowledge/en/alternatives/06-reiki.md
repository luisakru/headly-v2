# ✋ Reiki

Reiki comes from Japan. The practitioner gently lays their hands on the body or holds them just above it, usually at the head, shoulders and belly. According to Reiki, "universal life energy" flows during this. A session often lasts 30 to 60 minutes; you lie down, clothed and calm.

## What the studies say

There are no studies specifically on migraine yet. For pain in general, a Cochrane review found a small relief from touch therapies such as Reiki, and the largest effects even came from Reiki studies. Most studies are small and methodologically weak, though. An energy that could be measured has never been shown.

What certainly works: calm, a darkened room, gentle touch and someone who takes their time. This calms the nervous system, and that is exactly what can do a brain in an attack good. Whether you call it "energy" or relaxation may not matter much in the moment.

## What to look out for

- Reiki is an addition, not a replacement for acute medication or prevention
- Health insurance doesn't pay for it
- Serious practitioners don't promise a cure
- Use Headly to see whether it helps you, e.g. as your own event

**Our verdict:** hardly backed by science, but gentle, free of side effects and a real help in an attack for some.

## My experience

Reiki once helped me enormously during an acute attack.
