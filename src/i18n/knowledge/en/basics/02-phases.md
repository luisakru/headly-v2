# 🌘 The phases of an attack

An attack often starts long before the headache and ends only some time after it. Not every attack has every phase, and they can overlap.

![The four phases: prodrome, aura, headache, postdrome](phases)

## Early warning signs

**Hours to two days before.** Tiredness, yawning, cravings, a stiff neck, irritability, sensitivity to light or needing to pee often. Many people only notice them once they look out for them.

## Aura

**Usually 5 to 60 minutes**, in about one in four people. The aura can affect all senses, not just sight:

- Sight: flickering, zigzag lines, blind spots
- Touch: tingling or numbness, often from the hand up the arm into the face
- Speech: finding or saying words is hard
- Hearing, smell, taste: rarer, e.g. rushing sounds or phantom smells

Several symptoms usually come one after another, not at the same time.

## Headache

**Untreated 4 to 72 hours.** Often one-sided and throbbing, worse with movement, with nausea and sensitivity to light and sound. If it lasts longer than 72 hours, see "Status migrainosus".

## Recovery

**Up to a day or two afterwards.** Exhausted, unfocused, like a hangover. Some people also feel unusually euphoric. Allow yourself rest during this time, otherwise the next attack can come sooner.

Knowing your early warning signs lets you act earlier and take medication in time.
