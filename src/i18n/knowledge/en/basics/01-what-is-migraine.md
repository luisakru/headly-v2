# 🧠 What is migraine?

Migraine is a neurological disease, not a "normal" headache. About one in seven people has it, women about three times as often as men. The World Health Organization counts it among the conditions that cost the most years lived with disability worldwide.

## More than a headache

An untreated attack lasts 4 to 72 hours. The pain is often one-sided, throbbing and gets worse with movement. It usually comes with nausea and sensitivity to light, sound or smells. That is why Headly is kept so calm and dark.

But the headache is only one part. An attack can start hours earlier with yawning, cravings, a stiff neck or irritability, and linger for a day afterwards like a hangover. More on this in "The phases of an attack".

## Forms many people don't know

- **Migraine with aura:** about a quarter to a third have an aura before the pain, usually for 5 to 60 minutes. It can affect all senses: flickering or blind spots, tingling or numbness, trouble speaking, more rarely sounds or smells
- **Aura without headache:** the aura comes, the pain doesn't. Especially the first time, have it checked by a doctor, because it can look like a stroke
- **Vestibular migraine:** dizziness, swaying or spinning, often without any headache. It often goes unrecognised as migraine for a long time
- **Abdominal migraine:** mainly in children, with tummy pain and nausea instead of headache. Many get "classic" migraine later
- **Chronic migraine:** headache on 15 or more days a month, at least 8 of them with migraine. It has its own treatments

## What happens in the body

Migraine often runs in families; many genes each add a small part. They make the brain more open to stimuli: it processes light, sound and change more intensely and loses its balance more easily. An attack is not fully understood yet, but these steps are well supported:

![How an attack unfolds in the brain: hypothalamus, cortex, trigeminal nerve, meninges, brainstem and thalamus](attack)

1. **The hypothalamus gets out of step.** This control centre regulates sleep, hunger and hormones. Hours before the pain, it already shows unusual activity in scans. That is where early signs like yawning or cravings come from.
2. **With an aura, a wave runs across the cortex.** Nerve cells fire strongly for a moment and then go quiet. The wave moves slowly, about 3 millimetres a minute. That is why flickering or tingling spreads gradually.
3. **The trigeminal nerve is activated.** Its fine fibres wrap around the meninges and their blood vessels. Once activated, they release messengers, above all CGRP.
4. **The meninges become irritated.** CGRP widens the vessels and makes the nerve endings sensitive. The brain itself feels no pain, but the meninges do. The throbbing comes from here.
5. **Brainstem and thalamus amplify the signal.** They pass the pain on and turn up sensitivity to light, noise and smells. If this lasts long, they become oversensitive themselves, and then even touch hurts.

Migraine used to be called a blood vessel disease. Today we know: the vessels join in, but it starts in the nerves. The new medications target exactly this CGRP, see "Prevention" and "Triptans".

## What often helps

Migraine brains like routine. It sounds boring and every doctor says it, but it works:

- Regular sleep, weekends included
- Regular meals and enough to drink
- Endurance exercise such as cycling, swimming or walking
- Relaxation, for example progressive muscle relaxation
- A diary like Headly to spot patterns

Not everything helps everyone. Try one thing at a time and give it a few weeks.
