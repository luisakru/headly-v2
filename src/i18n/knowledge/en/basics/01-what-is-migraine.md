# 🧠 What is migraine?

Migraine is a neurological disease, not a "normal" headache. About one in seven people has it, women about three times as often as men. The World Health Organization counts it among the conditions that cost the most years lived with disability worldwide.

## More than a headache

An untreated attack lasts 4 to 72 hours. The pain is often one-sided, throbbing and gets worse with movement. It usually comes with nausea and sensitivity to light, sound or smells. That is why Headly is kept so calm and dark.

But the headache is only one part. An attack can start hours earlier with yawning, cravings, a stiff neck or irritability, and linger for a day afterwards like a hangover. More on this in "The phases of an attack".

## Forms many people don't know

- **Migraine with aura:** about a quarter to a third have an aura before the pain, usually for 5 to 60 minutes. It can affect all senses: flickering or blind spots, tingling or numbness, trouble speaking, more rarely sounds or smells
- **Aura without headache:** the aura comes, the pain doesn't. Especially the first time, have it checked by a doctor, because it can look like a stroke
- **Vestibular migraine:** dizziness, swaying or spinning, often without any headache. It often goes unrecognised as migraine for a long time
- **Abdominal migraine:** mainly in children, with tummy pain and nausea instead of headache. Many get "classic" migraine later
- **Chronic migraine:** headache on 15 or more days a month, at least 8 of them with migraine. It has its own treatments

## Where it comes from

Migraine often runs in families; many genes each add a small part. During an attack, nerve fibres around the meninges are activated and release messengers such as CGRP. That is exactly where the new medications work, see "Preventing with medication".
