# 🩸 Menstrual migraine

For many women, migraine clusters around their period. The likely trigger is the drop in oestrogen shortly before bleeding starts.

The international headache classification (ICHD-3) describes two forms:

- **Menstrually related migraine:** attacks around the first day of the period in at least two out of three cycles, and also at other times. This is the common form
- **Pure menstrual migraine:** attacks only around the period, never otherwise. This is rare

"Around" means from two days before to two days after the first day of bleeding. Bleeding in the pill-free week counts too.

## How to find out

Log the first day of your period for at least three cycles. In Headly, that is the small event "Period". Once there is enough data, Headly tells you whether your migraine fits this pattern. You can show the history to your doctor.

## What can help

These attacks often last longer and respond less well to medication, so it is worth talking to your doctor about them specifically. Possible approaches:

- Take acute medication early, as with any attack
- Short-term prevention for the days around the period, e.g. with a long-acting triptan or a pain reliever such as naproxen
- Hormonal approaches, e.g. taking the pill without a break so the oestrogen drop does not happen

Important: with migraine with aura, contraception containing oestrogen is usually not recommended because it raises the risk of stroke. This always belongs with a doctor.
