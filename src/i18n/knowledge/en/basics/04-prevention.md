# 🛡️ Prevention

A preventive is not taken during an attack but regularly, so that attacks become rarer and milder. It is usually worth it from three attacks a month, earlier if attacks are very severe or long. Roughly halving your migraine days already counts as success.

## The classic options

These medications were originally developed for other conditions and also work against migraine:

- **Beta blockers** such as metoprolol or propranolol, originally for high blood pressure
- **Flunarizine**, originally for dizziness
- **Topiramate**, originally for epilepsy
- **Amitriptyline**, originally for depression

They help many people but often have noticeable side effects such as tiredness, weight gain, dizziness or trouble concentrating. You start with a low dose and increase slowly. Whether it works shows only after about two to three months.

## The new options

They are the first medications developed specifically against migraine. They slow down the messenger CGRP, which plays a central part in an attack, and usually have few side effects.

- **CGRP antibodies:** an injection under the skin, once a month or every three months depending on the product. One is also given as an infusion
- **Gepants for prevention** such as atogepant (Aquipta): one tablet a day
- **Botox:** for chronic migraine, about every three months, many small injections in the forehead, head and neck

## Without medication

Prevention doesn't always have to be a tablet. According to the guideline, these approaches work well, alone or together with medication:

- **Endurance exercise,** e.g. three times a week for 30 minutes of cycling, swimming or brisk walking
- **Relaxation** such as progressive muscle relaxation, practised regularly
- **Biofeedback,** where you learn to consciously influence body signals such as muscle tension
- **Behavioural therapy,** to deal differently with stress and the fear of the next attack

Then there are magnesium, vitamin B2 and Q10 as well as acupuncture, see the articles under "Alternative approaches".

## How long?

If prevention works well, after about 6 to 12 months you discuss with your doctor whether a break is possible. For some, migraine stays less frequent afterwards; for others it comes back. Then you can start again.

## The hurdle in Germany

Statutory health insurance usually pays for the new options only once the classic ones have not worked, were not tolerated or are not suitable. That often means months of side effects before you get what might have helped straight away. So keep good track of what you tried, for how long and why you stopped. Headly does this with preventions in Profile, including the reason for stopping, and the PDF report shows it to your doctor.

## My experience

I also had to try the classic medications first, with all their side effects. That was hard. Then Aquipta helped me very, very, very much. If you are in the middle of this path right now: it is worth sticking with it.
