# About migraine

Not medical advice. Always discuss changes to your medication with your doctor.

## What is migraine?

Migraine is a neurological disease, not an "ordinary" headache. About one in seven people has it, and women about three times as often as men.

- An untreated attack lasts 4 to 72 hours
- The pain is often one-sided, throbbing and moderate to severe
- Physical activity makes it worse
- It comes with nausea or sensitivity to light and sound

## The phases of an attack

- Warning signs: hours to days before, such as tiredness, yawning, food cravings or a stiff neck
- Aura: in about one in four people, mostly visual like flickering or zigzag lines, 5 to 60 minutes
- Headache: the attack itself
- Recovery: often exhausted afterwards, "like a hangover"

## Acute medication

Taking it early usually works best. Common intervals for adults, as stated in the package leaflet:

- Ibuprofen 400 mg: at least 6 hours apart, at most 1,200 mg a day
- Paracetamol 500 mg: at least 6 hours apart, at most 4,000 mg a day
- Triptans such as sumatriptan: a second dose no sooner than 2 hours later, and only if the migraine comes back

If the first triptan dose does not help at all, a second one in the same attack usually won't either.

## The 10-day rule

Too many painkillers can cause headaches themselves. Take acute medication on no more than 10 days a month. The stats in Headly show your medication days.

## When to see a doctor

Right away, with:

- A sudden, extremely severe headache
- Weakness, trouble speaking or confusion you don't know from your aura
- Fever with a stiff neck
- A headache after a fall or a blow to the head

Soon, if:

- Your headaches change noticeably
- You have more than 3 attacks a month, as prevention may help
- You need medication on more than 10 days a month

## What often helps

- Regular sleep, weekends included
- Regular meals and enough to drink
- Endurance exercise such as cycling, swimming or walking
- Relaxation, for example progressive muscle relaxation
- A headache diary to spot patterns
